# STORY RULES
Rules specific to Respawn: 2006 that the planner and the continuity checker enforce.

## Future knowledge (Lin Chen's only "power")
- Lin Chen remembers his ORIGINAL life (2006-2022), imperfectly: big events, famous players and teams, roughly when AotA patches and AotA 2 came, the general shape of strategies that became popular. He does NOT remember exact dates, scores, prices or numbers, except things every Sichuanese knows (the earthquake: May 12, 2008, 14:28). When he leans on a memory he hedges ("sometime that spring", "I think").
- The butterfly effect: the more he changes, the less his knowledge fits. People he influenced make different choices; events near him drift. Big world events (earthquake, Olympics, crisis, Halcyon/AotA 2) still happen.
- Lin Chen's magazine column ("Qingshui Laoren" in Game Frontline) spreads his strategies: once printed, rivals can use them too, and later chapters should show other teams copying them. Because of this he stops writing the column (planned for Arc 3, after a rival beats Starlight with his own published strategy).
- Patches change the game: a strategy from a later version may not work in the current one. AotA 2 (2011) is the game of his whole pro career in the original life (2011-2022): there his knowledge is deepest and he shines the most (arc 7). Patches, the butterfly effect and rivals copying him still limit it, and the old temptation comes back with it: to become the selfish star who carries alone.
- Hometown knowledge: Lin Chen grew up in Qingshui and played at Starlight for two years in his original life. He knows the town, Starlight's layout (including the back room and back door) and most people's faces, names and futures. Every chapter should feel like a regression: he recognizes, compares past and future versions, anticipates, and chooses differently. Writing him as a stranger to his own town or cafe is a HIGH issue (knowledge-leak in reverse).
- His hands are 15. Mechanical skill (APM, last-hitting, reflexes) must be trained on the page; his edge is reading, timing, drafting, leadership.
- No system, no stat windows, no luck that solves a problem. Every win must come from things readers already saw: training, a plan, a known hero/item rule, a teammate's established strength.
- Money: Lin Chen's real income is game money, earned slowly (tournament prizes, paid strategy guides for gaming magazines, coaching, later sponsors and salaries). In his original life he ignored stocks, business and news; he only vaguely remembers things that "blew up" (the stock boom and crash, Bitcon, a few famous companies) and never when or which. His get-rich attempts are a running comedy subplot that always fails because he remembers wrong (wrong year, wrong company, buys at the top, searches for Bitcon before it exists). They never solve a problem, cost little, and appear rarely (about once every 15-20 chapters).
- Bitcon did not exist before January 2009. Chapter 3 plants it: he searches for it, finds nothing, and writes "BITCON?? (watch for it)" in his notebook. Before 2009, searches find nothing.
- Bitcon (the one that works, quietly): in early 2009 (arc 4), after he spots a tiny forum post about a new "digital money" and remembers his note, one late-night scene at Starlight with Monk, Lin Chen mines a modest amount of Bitcon on the old PCs, unsure whether he has already missed the timing. He writes the key on a scrap of paper ("Maybe this will be useful someday") and genuinely forgets it; Xu Qing tapes it into her team notebook "for the records". During the story it is never used, sold or checked, and no character treats it as valuable. Readers know Bitcon exploded, so play it as dramatic irony: two or three times across the story (spaced arcs apart, never before the 2009 mining scene) the scrap nearly gets destroyed by accident (Big Bird uses the notebook page as a coaster, Xu Qing nearly throws out "old junk" when moving, Fang Hu doodles on it), survives by luck, and nobody notices. Never wink at it in narration. What happens with it after the ending: to be decided with the user later.

## The loop (the central mystery)
- Lin Chen believes this is his first and only return. Before the arc-5 midpoint (around ch 180) he never suspects a loop; he explains fragments away (talent, luck, déjà vu).
- Fragments from erased loops come only as muscle memory, déjà vu, dreams, and knowing small things he never learned in his original life. They never give clear information before the midpoint. After it, memories return only when something in the present strongly matches a lost-loop moment (same place, same words, same song), and each memory is partial.
- Boss Mei knows Lin Chen has his original-life memories (it happens every loop) and that he has forgotten the loops; Lin Chen does not know she knows.
- Boss Mei remembers everything but only hints. Before arc 5 she never says it plainly, and no POV (including hers) states the loop, her memories, or her motive.
- Before arc 8: no one learns, says or suggests that Fang Hu is Mei's son, and the loop's cause stays hidden. Public story: Mei and Fang Hu are a cranky cafe owner and an annoying kid.
- Fang Hu's heart condition: only small physical clues before arc 9 (dizziness, breathlessness, pressing his chest, laughing it off). No diagnosis before arc 9.
- See macro_arcs.md for the lost loops and their order of reveal.
- Why he can only save Fang Hu at the end: he is NOT limited to a final window. He acts the whole story, but Fang Hu's loneliness only comes to a head near the 2012 final. The rule readers must learn early (arcs 1-2): you can't save someone with one act; you save them by staying. Plant it with a line from Grandma or Mei about showing up once versus staying, and with an early scene where a big fix-it act by Lin Chen fails.
- What readers may guess and what they may not: attentive readers may suspect early that Mei knows more than she says (that is fine and intended). Hold back what they cannot guess: what each failed loop looked like, what the wish cost Mei, how Fang Hu died each time, and that Fang Hu is her son (arc 8).

## Loop ledger (update after every chapter that plants a loop clue)
- Lin Chen remembers: his ORIGINAL life (2006-2022) only, imperfectly. From erased loops: nothing as memory before the midpoint; only body memory (hands that know a fix or a combo), déjà vu, dreams, and small facts he never learned.
- Mei remembers: the original life and every loop, fully.
- What resets: everything, on December 20, 2022, if Fang Hu has died by then; everyone's memories except Mei's. Lin Chen wakes on the morning of September 2, 2006.
- What a trigger releases (from the midpoint on): one partial lost-loop memory, only when the present strongly matches a lost-loop moment (same place, words, object or song).
- Clues planted so far (Ch: clue -> what it really is, author's note):
  - Ch 1: the calendar page (Friday, Sept 1) and the feeling he has read it "more than once" -> he has woken to this page seven times.
  - Ch 2: Mei holds a tile a second too long when he walks in; "You came. Eat something first." -> she has waited for this morning seven times.
  - Ch 8: Monk's pink bicycle bell with a rabbit; Lin Chen "knows" the name Ling -> a fact from a lost loop.
  - Ch 9: his hands find Mei's pliers and fix the bent chain link by feel -> body memory from a lost loop.
  - Ch 16: Mei says "Hu" (a mahjong win call, out of turn) while Fang Hu counts coins -> her son's name slipping out.
  - Ch 21 (Mei POV): she keeps a worn seven-of-dots tile in her cardigan pocket and turns it face up, then down -> seven loops. She flinches at the window when Lin Chen yanks Fang Hu back from a motorbike -> Loop 3 (he protected the boy too hard). She waits for him every morning and does not know where he is for once -> her one blind spot is what this loop changed.
  - Ch 23: at the county hospital his feet know the second registration window, the landing window over the river and the back-courtyard shortcut, and twice turn him toward the internal medicine corridor -> from loops where Fang Hu was a patient there (heart). Grandma notices the second time.
- Clue cadence (author decision, ch 21): at least one loop clue every 5 chapters that a reader can feel is "wrong" (a small thing that does not add up), even if they cannot explain it. Still nothing clear before the midpoint. Log each one above.
- Clues to plant later (small, spaced out, nothing clear before the midpoint):
  - He hums a tune Fang Hu made up; Fang Hu: "That's mine. Where did you hear it?"
  - Someone says "after the final" and he goes cold for no reason (sets up Loop 6).
  - A dream of a hotel corridor and a closed door.
  - His hand goes to Fang Hu's wrist to check his pulse before he thinks about it.

## Boss Mei: the cost of keeping the secret (for her scenes and POV chapters)
- She nearly tells him, starts with his name, and turns it into a scolding.
- She has to watch him make a mistake she has seen before (refusing kid money, deciding for Fang Hu) and say nothing.
- She flinches when Lin Chen grabs Fang Hu to protect him: she knows where that leads (Loop 3).
- Later: her son never calls her Ma and there is nobody she can tell; she talks to the white dragon tile.
- Her POV stays truthful but incomplete: what she does, notices and feels, never what she knows (before the arc-5 midpoint).

## Recurring gags and character rules
- The Typhoon secret: people who don't know Typhoon is a girl need a reason (she hides her face, uses the fake voice, nobody looks at the corner). Every scene that plays the secret for comedy also shows what it would cost Xiaoyu to be found out, so it never relies only on everyone being slow.
- Lin Chen's overprotective instinct (deciding for others, shielding Fang Hu, "no kid money") is a real flaw: show it in small ways every few chapters, sometimes caught or resented by others. The healthy alternative is giving someone a role, not shelter (as with Fang Hu's "drinks man" job in Ch 12). This is the flaw behind Loop 3 and arc 6.
- Day numbers never appear in prose (see the style guide).
- Prose texture (author decision, ch 23): a sentence that explains what an action, object or line already showed (its meaning or why it mattered) is a LOW issue, quote it so it can be cut. Many walk-on characters each with a perfect quirk and a perfect line in one chapter is a LOW issue.
- Running gags must change a little each time (author decision, ch 21): the same gag is never used the same way twice. Xu Qing's notebook entries slowly become her evidence for the "Who are you?" confrontation (each new entry about Lin Chen should be one she can later read back to him); Lin Chen's old-man sighs and Fang Hu being chased out of Starlight should shift with what is happening (who notices, what it costs, a new twist). A running gag that only repeats is a LOW issue.

## Dates
- Every plan states the chapter's exact in-world day on its first line: "Day N (Weekday, Month D, YYYY), time of day". Count from Day 1 = Saturday, September 2, 2006, and keep it consistent with the state's Date, Scheduled and Past days lines. Remember school runs Monday to Saturday.

## Era and tone
- Nothing appears before its time (smartphones, WeTalk, Weibu, livestreaming). A HIGH issue if presented as normal; fine only as Lin Chen's slip that others don't understand.
- Real tragedies (the earthquake) are never played for laughs or used as a simple win for Lin Chen. His warnings help some people, not all, and cost him something (disbelief, trouble at school, suspicion).
- No romance or anything sexual involving anyone under 18. Crushes are comedy; Lin Chen deflects.
- Lin Chen knows only what he learned on the page or in his original life. The state's Knowledge Gaps list is binding; using anything on it is a HIGH issue.
- First-life private facts (author decision, ch 22): Lin Chen left Qingshui at 17 and never knew other people's private struggles there. Narration that states such a first-life fact as certain (how long Grandma's knee hurt, someone's illness or money trouble, old prices) without a hedge and without canon support is a MEDIUM issue (state-guess).
- Skin tone described by comparison to food or drink: LOW issue.
- Real brand names (Nokia, QQ, Apple, Coca-Cola...) are never used; only the look-alike names in the world bible. A real brand on the page is a LOW issue. Use the look-alike names plainly: name the brand once in a scene, then a plain word (the cola, his phone); never make a joke out of the name itself. Bitcon is the exception only because it is a plot device.

## Language: American English only
- All prose, dialogue, summaries, state and canon use American spelling AND American vocabulary. A British word or spelling is a LOW issue (checker: quote it and give the American word).
- Common slips to catch: flat (apartment), fringe (bangs), biscuit (cookie), term (semester), maths (math), toilet as the room (bathroom), tap (faucet), pavement (sidewalk), trousers (pants), rubbish (trash/garbage), full stop (period), queue (line), mobile (cell phone), timetable (schedule), lunch tin (lunch box), lift (elevator), torch (flashlight), petrol (gas), holiday as a trip (vacation), autumn (fall; but the festival stays "Mid-Autumn"), mum (mom), "was sat/was stood" (was sitting/standing), -our, -re, -ise, -ll- spellings (color, center, realize, traveled), grey (gray), towards (toward), whilst, amongst, learnt.
- Mahjong call: "peng" (Chinese), not "pung".
- Spoken language: the default is Chinese (local Sichuan dialect). Any line spoken in another language (English, Russian, Cantonese...) must be marked in the prose (said "in English" etc.) and set in italics. An unmarked foreign-language line is a LOW issue.
