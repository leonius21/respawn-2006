On Tuesday, October 3, Lin Chen takes Grandma to the county hospital, where his feet uncannily know the second registration window, the landing window over the river, the back-courtyard shortcut and twice turn him toward the internal medicine corridor (he covers with "Qiu Fang told me"; Grandma notices the second time and says nothing); he trades the stall key back for her agreeing to an X-ray, and Doctor Tian diagnoses worn cartilage and a strained ligament: no operation, brace, cane, six weeks without lifting or standing all day, recheck in a month, 126 yuan paid from the handkerchief roll. In the courtyard Grandma says "Your mother will cry," and when he says he'll call tonight she doesn't forbid it; that evening he dials the Dongguan dormitory himself, where kind foreman Lao Ou fetches his father from the shower and his mother from Building Four, and after tears, anger at Grandma and an argument about coming home, he asks them not to come or send money but to call Grandma tomorrow and be angry in their own words. His father says "You did right to call. Don't skip school," and Grandma, who heard from the doorway, puts a hot bowl of rice in his hands: "Your father's voice shook. Eat."
