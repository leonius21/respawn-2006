# WORLD BIBLE (~1300 words)

**Setting:** the real world, real China, starting Saturday, September 2, 2006. Real cities, real history. Real brands are replaced by look-alike names (see "Brands" below). Only the games, game companies, esports teams, tournaments and platforms are invented. The story spans 2006 to summer 2012 and goes international from 2011.

**Hometown:** Qingshui (清水镇), a fictional small county town in Deyang prefecture, Sichuan, about two hours by bus from Chengdu. One main street, a river, a market, a junior and a senior middle school, mahjong parlors, three net cafes. People speak Sichuan dialect at home and Mandarin at school. Many children are "left-behind kids" raised by grandparents while their parents work in Guangdong factories.
- **Starlight Net Cafe** (星光网吧): Boss Mei's cafe above a hardware shop on the main street. About 40 old PCs with CRT monitors, a broken air conditioner, a fridge of soft drinks and instant noodles, smoke in the air, a back room for hiding minors. 2 yuan an hour, 8 yuan for an overnight package.

**Daily life in 2006 (era rules):**
- Phones: Novia and Motocoola phones, cheap flip phones, SMS; QiQi chat on PCs. No smartphones (the Pear Phone launches in the US in 2007, in China in 2009). Weibu (microblog) is 2009, WeTalk (phone chat app) 2011. Nothing that doesn't exist yet may appear as normal; when Lin Chen slips and mentions one, it's a joke and people stare.
- Internet: slow ADSL, net cafes everywhere. Online video is new; tournaments are watched in person or as downloaded replays.
- Minors are officially banned from net cafes (since 2002). Teens sneak in with borrowed adult ID cards. The police and the culture bureau do inspections; cafes hide minors under desks or in back rooms.
- Parents call games "electronic heroin". "Internet addiction" boot camps with harsh treatment exist.
- School: junior middle school ends with the **zhongkao** (high school entrance exam, mid-June); senior high ends with the **gaokao** (university exam, June 7-8). School runs six days a week, with evening self-study for older students.
- Money (yuan): a bowl of noodles 3-4; net cafe 2 per hour; a bus ticket to Chengdu 30; a factory worker's monthly wage about 1,000-1,500. Lin Chen's parents send home 800 a month. Grandma's pickle stall makes a few hundred a month.

**Brands (always use these look-alike names, never the real ones; invent new ones the same way and add them to canon):**
- Phones and tech: Novia (Nokia), Motocoola (Motorola), Samsong (Samsung), Sonny Erikson (Sony Ericsson), Lenovia (Lenovo), Pear and its Pear Phone (Apple, iPhone).
- Internet: QiQi (QQ, the PC chat app with a penguin-like chick mascot), Taobei (Taobao, online shopping), Baidoo (Baidu, search), Weibu (Weibo, from 2009), WeTalk (WeChat, from 2011), Bitcon (Bitcoin, digital money, from January 2009).
- Food and drink: Master Kang-style noodles are "Uncle Kong" instant noodles; Coca-Cola is "Coco Cola"; Red Bull is "Red Ox".
- Sports and fashion: Nike is "Nyke", Adidas is "Adidaz"; basketball jerseys carry invented player names.

**Chinese culture and daily life (use naturally, never as a lecture):**
- *Festivals:* National Day "golden week" Oct 1-7 (travel, crowded buses, cafes full). Mid-Autumn Festival (mooncakes, family reunion, the moon): Oct 6, 2006; Sept 25, 2007. Spring Festival (red envelopes of money, firecrackers, the CCTV Gala on TV, relatives asking about grades and comparing children, the only time migrant parents come home): Feb 18, 2007; Feb 7, 2008; Jan 26, 2009; Feb 14, 2010; Feb 3, 2011; Jan 23, 2012. The Lantern Festival ends it 15 days later. Qingming (around April 5: sweeping family graves, burning paper money). Dragon Boat (zongzi rice dumplings): June 19, 2007. Qingming, Dragon Boat and Mid-Autumn become public holidays from 2008.
- *Family:* "face" (mianzi): never shame the family in public. Filial duty to elders. Parents compare their child with "other people's child" (别人家的孩子), who always has better grades. Love is shown with food and money, rarely with words; "Have you eaten?" means "I care about you". Teen dating ("early love", 早恋) is banned by schools and feared by parents.
- *Address:* Ge (older brother), Jie (older sister), Didi and Meimei (younger brother/sister) are also used for friends. Kids call adults Ayi (auntie) and Shushu (uncle), and old people Nainai/Yeye (grandma/grandpa). "Lao" (old) and "Xiao" (little) go before surnames between friends and coworkers (Lao Zhao, Xiao Lin). The cafe's attendant is the wangguan (网管); everyone calls Monk that.
- *School:* tracksuit uniforms; morning broadcast exercises to loudspeaker music and eye exercises twice a day; exam rankings posted on the wall for all to see; the homeroom teacher (banzhuren) is the most powerful adult in a kid's life and calls parents. Primary pupils like Fang Hu wear the red scarf of the Young Pioneers. Students clean the classroom in turns.
- *Sichuan life:* tea houses with bamboo chairs, mahjong everywhere, hotpot, dandan noodles, spicy rabbit heads, night street food (skewers, cold noodles), Sichuan pepper that numbs the mouth. Sichuan people are famous for loving comfort and leisure ("bashi!" = great, comfy). Rain and gray skies; hot, wet summers; damp, cold winters with no central heating (people wear coats indoors).
- *Beliefs:* temples and incense, fortune-telling, 8 lucky and 4 unlucky (sounds like "death"). Funerals: white clothes, paper offerings, a mourning hall, the seventh-day rites. Older people are quietly superstitious even if they deny it.
- *Gamer culture (2006):* overnight packages (baoye, 包夜) from 10 p.m. to 8 a.m.; instant noodles with a ham sausage, spicy strips (latiao) and Coco Cola; a beginner is a "vegetable bird" (cainiao, 菜鸟), a top player a "great god" (dashen, 大神), a second account a "small account". "gg" at the end of a game. Smoke, shouting across rows, headsets shared and sticky keyboards.

**Real events the story uses (handle with respect, never as spectacle):**
- **May 12, 2008, 14:28:** the Wenchuan (Sichuan) earthquake, nearly 70,000 dead. Qingshui is shaken and damaged but not destroyed; the mountain towns nearby are hit far worse. Every Sichuanese remembers the date and the minute, so Lin Chen does too.
- **August 8-24, 2008:** the Beijing Olympics.
- **Late 2008:** the financial crisis closes many Guangdong factories. Lin Chen's parents lose their jobs and come home.

**The games:**
- **Peacecraft III** (2002) by **Snowstorm Entertainment**: a fantasy real-time strategy game. Its map editor lets fans make custom maps.
- **AotA, "Attack of the Ancients":** a free custom map (mod) for Peacecraft III, kept updated by an anonymous mapmaker known only as **"Lantern"**. Versions are numbered (6.3x in autumn 2006). Each new version ("patch") changes heroes and items.
- **Platforms:** Chinese players play AotA online through fan platforms such as **Lianzhan** (联战平台, "Linked Battle"), with ranks and rooms, or over LAN in cafes.
- **AotA 2:** in 2010, Lantern joins **Halcyon Software**, which announces AotA 2, a standalone game on a new engine. Closed beta in 2011. Halcyon's **World Championship** (first prize US$1 million) is held in **Cologne, Germany, in August 2011** and in **Shanghai in August 2012** (the story's final).

**Game Frontline** (游戏前线): a fictional national monthly PC gaming magazine from Beijing, sold at newsstands and read cover to cover in every net cafe. It pays about 100 yuan per thousand characters for strategy articles, by postal money order a few weeks after printing. Lin Chen writes AotA strategy for it under the pen name **"Qingshui Laoren"** (清水老人, "the Old Man of Qingshui"), so readers imagine an old master; nobody knows it is a 15-year-old.

**Esports scene by year (this timeline):**
- 2006: cafe tournaments (a few hundred yuan or free hours as prizes) and city LANs. No salaries.
- 2007: city circuits (Chengdu, Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou) and a national online cup. Prizes in the thousands, up to tens of thousands of yuan.
- 2008-2010: the first clubs with tiny salaries, **team houses** (crowded flats where players live and train), sponsors (electronics companies), national finals in Shanghai. International LANs begin (Southeast Asia, Russia, Europe).
- 2011-2012: AotA 2 and the World Championships. Real money arrives.
- **Golden Wolf Club** (金狼): signs minors for almost nothing, makes them train 14+ hours a day, and dumps them when they break. Owner: **Hou Desheng**. In the original life, Fang Hu died while playing for them.

**AotA rules (hard rules; the game never bends for the plot):**
- 5 vs 5. Two sides: **Dawn** (bottom-left) and **Dusk** (top-right). Each defends its **Ancient**; destroying the enemy Ancient wins. Games last 25-60 minutes.
- Three lanes (top, middle, bottom), each with three towers per side and barracks at the base; a jungle with neutral monsters; a river across the middle.
- **Creeps** (small soldiers) march down each lane. The killing blow on a creep gives gold (**last-hitting**); killing your own weakened creep denies the enemy its gold (**denying**).
- Heroes gain experience and levels (max 25), learn 4 skills (the 4th, the ultimate, from level 6), and buy items with gold at shops. Dead heroes respawn after a timer that grows with level, and they lose some gold.
- **Runes** appear in the river every 2 minutes: Haste, Fury (double damage), Mirror (illusions), Veil (invisibility), Renewal (healing).
- **The Old Wyrm:** a strong neutral boss in a pit by the river. Killing it drops the **Wyrm's Heart**: the holder revives once on death.
- Players see only what their own side's units see (fog of war). **Watcher Eyes** (wards) give vision.
- Roles: carry (farms, wins late), mid (solo middle lane, the playmaker), offlane (tough, alone against two), supports (wards, protection, give up gold), captain (drafts heroes and calls plays; can be any role).
- In tournaments, captains ban and pick heroes in turns (the draft). A team may register a substitute, who can replace a player **between games**, never during one.
- Skill comes from last-hitting, map awareness, timing, positioning, teamwork, and hand speed (actions per minute, APM). A 15-year-old's hands, however smart the mind, need training.

**Heroes (look-alike names; skills always described in our own words; never use real names, skill names or text):**
- **Fudge the Butcher:** throws a chain hook that drags an enemy to him; a stench aura that hurts nearby enemies (and himself); grows tougher from kills nearby; ultimate: grabs one enemy and carves at them.
- **Timeless Void:** skips a short distance through time; sometimes dodges hits; ultimate **Stasis Sphere**: a dome that freezes everyone inside except himself, *friend and foe alike*. Lin Chen's first-life meme hero.
- **Soul Fiend (Neverdawn):** three "razes" that blast the ground at short, middle and long range; grows stronger from the souls of kills; ultimate: releases the souls in a ring. Lin Chen's first-life signature mid hero.
- **Anti-Magus:** blinks short distances; burns enemy mana with each hit; ultimate blasts a target for the mana it's missing.
- **Frost Maiden:** slows and freezes; a mana aura for her team; ultimate: an ice storm around her while she stands still.
- **Earthbreaker:** splits the ground into an impassable wall; stomps to stun; ultimate: a shockwave that echoes off every nearby unit.
- **Sand Lord:** burrows under enemies to stun them; ultimate: a series of sand pulses around him.
- **Riddle:** summons small minions; ultimate: a vortex that pulls enemies in and traps them.
- **Tidecaller:** a huge sea giant; ultimate: tentacles burst from the ground around him and stun everyone nearby.
- **Phantom Blade:** throws daggers, blinks to targets, dodges, lands huge critical hits.
- **Morphant:** a water creature that shifts between strength and agility, and can become a copy of an enemy hero.
- **Storm Sprite:** turns into lightning and flies across the map; ultimate drains mana as he travels.
- **Lyon the Hexer:** spikes that stun in a line; turns an enemy into a frog; ultimate: a finger of death that hits one target very hard.
- **Haunt:** her ultimate sends a ghost copy of her to every enemy hero on the map at once.

- **Ursar the Warbear:** a huge bear who claws faster and harder with each hit on the same target; slams the ground to slow; ultimate: a blind rage that makes him nearly unkillable for a few seconds. Kills the jungle boss early.

- **Vyper the Netherdrake:** a flying wyvern whose poisoned attacks slow and stack; his skin poisons whoever hits him; ultimate: a heavy poison on one target.
- **Lyna Firecaller:** a fire mage; a line of flame, a delayed pillar that stuns; ultimate: a single bolt of fire that ends a fight. Mid.
- **Zuse the Thunderlord:** an old god who throws lightning; ultimate strikes every enemy hero on the map at once, anywhere they are.
- **Tinkerer:** a tiny inventor in a mech; lasers, homing rockets; ultimate: instantly resets his other skills and items, so he can do everything twice.

- **Red Axe:** a red-skinned berserker; taunts every nearby enemy into hitting him and spins back at them; ultimate: executes an enemy below a health line. Offlane.
