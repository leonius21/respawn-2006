# Respawn: 2006

*An esports comedy-drama set in a small Sichuan town in 2006. No system, no stat screens: just memory, practice and stubbornness.*

Lin Chen was a pro gamer who never won the big one. He was famous for one thing: choking in a final so badly that "pulling a Second" became a meme. At 31, in December 2022, he died broke and alone. Then he woke up on the morning of September 2, 2006. He is fifteen again, in his grandmother's apartment behind the market in Qingshui. His grandmother is alive, and the game that ate his life (*Attack of the Ancients*, a fan-made map for *Peacecraft III*) is still something kids play in smoky net cafes for one yuan an hour.

He has an adult's mind, sixteen years of half-remembered future, and the clumsy hands of a kid. Last time he tried to win alone. This time he's building a team.

- **Status:** chapters 1-20 written and accepted (about 51,000 words). The story covers its first month, September 2 to October 1, 2006.
- **Plan:** 400 chapters in 10 arcs of 40, ending at the 2012 World Championship in Shanghai.
- **Where the chapters are:** `chapters/`. One-paragraph summaries are in `summaries/`.

## The story so far (chapters 1-20)

**Waking up (ch 1-3).** Lin Chen works out the date from a tear-off calendar and a glued flip phone, and cries into pickled-radish porridge he spent years trying to find again. Grandma gives him 10 yuan and orders him to stay away from the net cafe above the hardware shop, so of course he goes there. At Starlight Net Cafe, the owner, Boss Mei, gives him a free-hour card and says, oddly, "You came. Eat something first." His game sense is still there, but his fingers aren't: a Cola-sticky key costs him the game, and a loud eleven-year-old called Fang Hu mocks him to the whole room. The first call with his parents in Dongguan ends at seven minutes with "Study hard. Don't end up like us." That night he listens to Grandma snore through the wall and decides that if this is real, he's going to do it right.

**Finding the team (ch 4-10).** At school, the class monitor Xu Qing writes down his old-man sighs in a green notebook, marked "Suspicious." Su Wen, the top student, quietly counts game timers under his breath and sneaks to Starlight through the back door. Big Bird, the huge son of the beef-noodle shop, turns out to have perfect instincts for when to charge in, until his father drags him out by the ear. A hooded kid on machine 38 crushes Lin Chen 2-0 in a duel. Lin Chen knows "he" is He Xiaoyu, a girl from another class. She sets her price: beat her once, fairly, with his brain. During a police raid, the kids hide behind noodle boxes while Fang Hu's father, Officer Fang Jianguo, walks the rows. Lin Chen lets slip the name of Monk's little sister, Ling. Monk is the silent night-shift attendant, and he asks how Lin Chen knows her name. Fang Hu names the team "Starlight," which makes Boss Mei freeze. Xu Qing catches them all, then finds the rule that lets a cafe owner sign the tournament form and makes herself treasurer of a fund holding 6.50 yuan.

**Becoming a team (ch 11-20).** By not filling the silences on the phone, Lin Chen lets his father finish a whole sentence for the first time. The team's first real game, against the local kids led by Streak, is won with a trap built from the other side's habits, and Monk says his first full sentence to Lin Chen in either life. Su Wen's father mistakes their game-timer trade for tutoring and makes it a weekly study group. Lin Chen writes a strategy article for *Game Frontline* under the pen name "Qingshui Laoren," the Old Man of Qingshui, and Fang Hu salutes the post box. Xu Qing runs budget meetings with an agenda. Fang Hu empties his toffee tin of bottle money into the fund, and Lin Chen's reflex "No kid money" crushes him until they settle on three yuan with a real receipt. Fang Hu gets dizzy at the curb and laughs it off. Officer Fang takes Lin Chen on a walk along the river and asks only about his son. On October 1, the magazine accepts the article but will pay too late for the fee, Starlight shuts for four days without explanation, and Grandma's knee gives out under a crate of cabbage. Lin Chen carries her home on his back while she tells him that coming once is easy and staying is what matters.

**Open threads at Chapter 21:** the fund is at 11.60 of 75 yuan, due October 15. The duel with Xiaoyu stands at 1-2. Grandma sees a bone doctor on Tuesday. Midterms are October 12-13, and Lin Chen has promised top 25. Starlight is closed, and nobody will say why.

## My opinion of it

*Written by Claude, the model that plans and checks the chapters, after reading all twenty summaries, the planning notes and the prose. I'm not a neutral reader: I helped make this. Here is my honest view anyway.*

**What works best is that it isn't really about the game.** The time-travel-gamer premise is a well-worn web-novel setup, and the opening chapters could have been "pro wins everything again." Instead, the strongest scenes have nothing to do with AotA: the eleven-minute phone call where a father finishes a sentence, Grandma counting her grandson's breaths through the wall, a policeman crouching to retie a knotted shoelace instead of answering a question, a boy's essay that says only "My father is a policeman. He is at work." The story knows that a 31-year-old who failed at life wants a second chance at people more than at trophies. That gives it a heart most of the genre doesn't have.

**The adults are its secret weapon.** Grandma, Fang Jianguo and Boss Mei are all written as people with their own weight, not as obstacles or helpers. None of them is stupid, and each one is hiding something. Fang Jianguo in particular is a great character after only two real scenes. The rule that "adults aren't stupid" is paying off.

**The comedy comes from character, which is why it lands.** Xu Qing's green notebook, Big Bird's plans that collapse when he remembers the "kitchen" is his dad, Fang Hu as "drinks man," Mei charging people a yuan to "look at the ghost." These are funny because they come from who the characters are, not from punchlines. The money is a good plot engine too: 11.60 of 75 yuan is a stake every reader understands, and it keeps forcing small, concrete choices.

**Fang Hu is the long bet, and so far it's paying.** Readers aren't told he's Mei's son or that he dies in every loop. They just see a loud, gap-toothed kid who waves with his whole arm. The clues (the dizziness, the hand on his chest, Mei's flinch, the name "Starlight") are planted lightly enough to miss. When the midpoint reveal comes, the toffee-tin receipt scene is going to hurt in hindsight, which is exactly the point.

**My concerns, honestly:**

1. **Pace.** Twenty chapters cover one month, and the team has played one real game. The plan has Arc 1 reaching December by chapter 40, but at this speed it will barely reach the October 15 tournament. Slow and warm is this story's strength, but web-serial readers usually want a clear win every few chapters, and the tournament needs to arrive before patience runs out. My suggestion: let some days pass in a paragraph, not a chapter.
2. **Too many small gags in rotation.** The notebook, the old-man sighs and Fang Hu being chased out are all good, but they come back so often that they risk turning into tics. The new 5-chapter spacing rule should help.
3. **Xiaoyu and Monk are behind the others.** Xiaoyu has one strong POV chapter. Monk has mostly silence, which is the point of him, but he needs a scene that is his before Arc 2 asks readers to care about his mother's dialysis.
4. **The loop mystery is very well hidden, maybe a little too well.** The midpoint reveal is about 160 chapters away. A few more clues that readers can feel are wrong, without being able to explain them, would keep curious readers reading. The Boss Mei chapter (ch 21) and the hospital corridor scene are the right places for that.

**Overall:** it's a warm, funny, quietly sad book that is better than its premise. The writing is concrete: the right objects of 2006, the smells, the yuan and fen. It's carried by side characters who feel alive. If the pace picks up a little and the game stays secondary to the people, this has a real chance of finding loyal readers.
