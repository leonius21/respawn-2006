# Royal Road listing

## Synopsis

In 2015, in front of forty thousand people, Lin Chen froze his own team in the deciding fight of the World Championship final. By morning his gamer tag was a verb. "Pulling a Second" meant choking when it mattered most.

Seven years later he died the way he'd lived his last years: broke, alone, too proud to plug in the heater.

Then he woke up.

It's 2006. He's fifteen, in a small town in Sichuan, and his grandmother is alive and shouting at him to eat his porridge. Two streets away, in a smoky net cafe with forty dying computers and a NO CRYING sign, the game that ate his life is still a free fan-made map nobody takes seriously.

He has a 31-year-old mind, sixteen years of the future, and the hands of a kid who can't land a last-hit. He also has a second chance at the thing he never managed the first time: not winning alone, but building a team.

A noodle-shop giant who charges into everything. The top student who counts in his sleep. The best mid player in town, who hides her name under a hood. A silent night-shift attendant. A gap-toothed kid who won't stop following him.

And a cafe owner who looked up from her mahjong the first time he walked in, as if she'd been expecting him.

**This time, Second plays for keeps.**

---
*An esports comedy-drama set in real-world China, 2006. No system, no stat screens: only memory, practice and stubbornness. Expect net cafe raids, a grandmother who feeds everyone, a lot of jokes, and a mystery under it all.*

## Short version (for places with a character limit)

A washed-up pro gamer who became a meme for choking in the World Championship final dies broke at 31, and wakes up at 15 in a small Sichuan town in 2006, when his game is still a mod played in smoky net cafes. His grandmother is alive. His hands are clumsy. And this time he's going to build the team he never had. An esports comedy-drama with no system, a lot of heart, and a mystery under it all.
